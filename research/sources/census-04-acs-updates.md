## 2026

### Just Released: 2025 ACS 1-Year Estimates

_October 29, 2026_

We are pleased to release the **2025 American Community Survey (ACS) 1-year estimates and the 2025 ACS 1-year Supplemental Estimates**, the most relied-on source for up-to-date social, economic, housing, and demographic information.

ACS 1-year estimates are available for the nation, 50 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, congressional districts, metropolitan areas, and counties and places with a population of **65,000 or more**. Supplemental Estimates are simplified versions of popular ACS tables for geographic areas with **at least 20,000 people**.

To find out if these estimates are available for your geographic area, please consult our [Reference Materials](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/geography-acs/reference-materials.html).

Today's release includes:

- [Comparison Profiles](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/table?d=ACS+1-Year+Estimates+Comparison+Profiles)
- [Data Profiles](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/table?d=ACS+1-Year+Estimates+Data+Profiles)
- [Detailed Tables](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/table?d=ACS+1-Year+Estimates+Detailed+Tables)
- [Geographic Comparison Tables](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/data-tables/geographic-comparison-tables.html)
- [Ranking Tables](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/data-tables/ranking-tables.html)
- [Selected Population Profiles](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/table?d=ACS+1-Year+Estimates+Selected+Population+Profiles)
- [Subject Tables](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/table?d=ACS+1-Year+Estimates+Subject+Tables)
- [Summary File](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/summary-file.html)
- [Supplemental Estimates](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/data-tables/supplemental-tables.html)

Begin exploring the new estimates on [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/) or through the [Census Bureau’s Application Programming Interface (API)](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/acs-1year.html).

**Available Resources:**

- [**Comparison Guidance**](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/guidance/comparing-acs-data.html) **:** Understand how this year’s estimates compare to previous ACS estimates, the 2010 Census, and the 2020 Census.

**Celebrate Irish-American Heritage and St. Patrick's Day with ACS Data**

The U.S. Congress designated March as Irish-American Heritage Month in 1991, and the president issues a proclamation commemorating the occasion each year.

Originally a religious holiday to honor St. Patrick who introduced Christianity to Ireland in the 5th century, St. Patrick’s Day has evolved into a celebration of all things Irish. The world’s first St. Patrick’s Day parade occurred in New York City on March 17, 1762, featuring Irish soldiers who served in the English military. This parade became an annual event, with President Harry S. Truman attending in 1948. The following facts found in [Irish-American Heritage Month and St. Patrick's Day: March 2026](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/facts-for-features/2026/irish-american-heritage.html) are made possible by the invaluable responses to [Census Bureau surveys](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys.html). We appreciate the public’s cooperation as we continuously measure America’s people and economy.

### U.S. Census Bureau Releases 2020-2024 ACS 5-Year PUMS Files

_March 5, 2026_

We are pleased to announce the release of the 2020-2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year Public Use Microdata Sample (PUMS) files.

The ACS 5-year PUMS files show the full range of population and housing unit responses collected on individual ACS questionnaires for a subsample of ACS housing units and group quarters persons (approximately five percent of the United States population) with disclosure protection enabled so that individuals or housing units cannot be identified.

PUMS files allow data users to create custom estimates and tables that are not available through ACS pretabulated data products. PUMS data can be accessed via the [Microdata Access Tool](https://data.census.gov/app/mdat/ACSPUMS5Y2024 "Original URL: https://data.census.gov/app/mdat/. Click or tap if you trust this link."), or data users can opt to download PUMS datasets onto a local computer and analyze them using statistical software such as R, SPSS, Stata, or SAS.

PUMS data can be accessed via [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov/app/mdat/ACSPUMS5Y2024) and the Census Bureau's [FTP site](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/pums/2024/).

Visit our [Microdata section](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/microdata.html) on the ACS website to find all resources related to PUMS files.  This section contains access to PUMS data, the PUMS Handbook, How-to instructions, FAQs, and PUMS documentation.

The [PUMS Documentation](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/microdata/documentation.html) page is updated with the 2024 release to include the PUMS User Guide, PUMS Variable Changes and Explanations, Subjects in the PUMS, PUMS Code Lists, Data Dictionaries for PUMS Files, Microdata API Data Dictionaries, Accuracy of the PUMS, PUMS Top Coded and Bottom Coded Values, PUMS Estimates for User Verification, and PUMS Record Counts.

### February 2026 Updates

**_February 17, 2026_**

**2020-2024 American Community Survey 5-year Data Release**

On January 29, the U.S. Census Bureau released the [2020-2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year estimates and Variance Replicate Estimates](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-kits/2026/acs-5-year-estimates.html). **Invitation for Public Feedback for a Federal Register Notice**

The Census Bureau is inviting public feedback through a [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/19/2025-23329/agency-information-collection-activities-submission-to-the-office-of-management-and-budget-omb-for) (FRN) on proposed plans for the 2027 ACS and Puerto Rico Community Survey (PRCS). The Census Bureau is proposing to introduce an internet self-response option to the PRCS data collection operation and to include the updated race and ethnicity question in the ACS and PRCS, which follows the Office of Management and Budget's (OMB) 2024 [Statistical Policy Directive No. 15](https://spd15revision.gov/). Currently, race and ethnicity data are collected and tabulated following OMB's 1997 SPD 15 on Federal race and ethnicity data standards. The public may submit comments through TODAY, February 17, 2026. See the "Addresses" section of the FRN for instructions on how to submit comments.

**Data Product Releases Using ACS Data**

- [New Community Resilience Estimates](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2024-cre-natural-hazard-risk-tables.html) by social vulnerability to natural disasters are now available. This product helps local governments and community stakeholders plan mitigation and recovery strategies in the event of a disaster. Estimates were released for the nation, states, counties, core-based statistical areas and tracts.

- New state-level statistics on geographic mobility and place of birth in 2024 were released. [Geographic mobility](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/migration/about.html) refers to the movement of people within and across boundaries. The State-to-State Migration Flows table package contains:
  - **[State-to-State Migration Flows](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/geographic-mobility/state-to-state-migration.html)**. This table provides estimates of the number of people moving between the 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico and to the United States from abroad.
  - **[State of Residence by Place of Birth](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/geographic-mobility/state-of-residence-place-of-birth-acs.html)**. This table provides estimates of people currently living in the 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico by place of birth.

- The annual release of the [Characteristics of Same-Sex Couple Households: 2005-2024](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/same-sex-couples/ssc-house-characteristics.html) explores demographic and economic characteristics of same-sex couple households by age, race, ethnicity, educational attainment, employment status, income, and home tenure.

- The recently published [Social and Economic Characteristics of Currently Unmarried Women With a Recent Birth: 2023](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/acs/acs-62.html) report uses data from the ACS to explore social and economic characteristics of unmarried women with a birth in the last year.

- The [2024 Planning Database (PDB)](https://www.census.gov/topics/research/guidance/planning-databases.html), which includes the Low Response Score (LRS) and other new 2020 Census operational data was recently released. The 2024 PDB contains a selection of housing, demographic, socioeconomic and operational data for block groups and census tracts from the 2020 Census Redistricting Data (Public Law 94-171) Summary File; 2020 Census Demographic and Housing Characteristics File; and 2018-2022 American Community Survey 5-year estimates.

### U.S. Census Bureau Releases 2020-2024 ACS 5-Year Estimates

**_January 29, 2026_**

We are pleased to announce the release of the [2020-2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year estimates,](https://data.census.gov/table?q=acs&y=2024) the most relied-on source for up-to-date social, economic, housing, and demographic information every year. To access data from the link, select “Dataset” in the toolbar above the table, then choose “2024: ACS 5-year Estimates.” The ACS is the only source of local statistics for most of the [40+ topics](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/guidance/subjects.html) it covers, such as educational attainment, occupation, language spoken at home, nativity, veteran status, and selected monthly homeowner costs.

ACS 5-year estimates are available for most geographic areas regardless of population size, down to the block-group level. Today's 2020-2024 ACS 5-year release will also include all data products, consisting of [Comparison Profiles](https://data.census.gov/table?d=ACS+5-Year+Estimates+Comparison+Profiles), [Data Profiles](https://data.census.gov/table?d=ACS+5-Year+Estimates+Data+Profiles), [Detailed Tables](https://data.census.gov/table?d=ACS+5-Year+Estimates+Detailed+Tables), [Narrative Profiles](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/data-tables/narrative-profiles.html), [Subject Tables](https://data.census.gov/table?d=ACS+5-Year+Estimates+Subject+Tables) and the [Summary File](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/summary-file.html). Visit [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov/cedsci/) to start exploring these estimates on the Census Bureau’s primary data platform. The Census Bureau is inviting public feedback through a [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/19/2025-23329/agency-information-collection-activities-submission-to-the-office-of-management-and-budget-omb-for) (FRN) on proposed plans for the 2027 American Community Survey (ACS) and Puerto Rico Community Survey (PRCS). The Census Bureau is proposing to introduce an internet self-response option to the PRCS data collection operation and to include the updated race and ethnicity question in the ACS and the PRCS, which follows the Office of Management and Budget’s (OMB) 2024 [Statistical Policy Directive No. 15](https://spd15revision.gov/). Currently, race and ethnicity data are collected and tabulated following OMB’s 1997 SPD 15 on Federal race and ethnicity data standards. The public may submit comments through February 17, 2026. See the “Addresses” section of the FRN for instructions on how to submit comments.
